## Game, honest model and exact theorem
Suppose classical PPT Gen(x) returns a **uniform h-bit protected K** (retained only by the honest constructor during setup) and a classical full public view V. V includes the public mixed program, native VK(K), setup transcript, all fixed auxiliary public outputs, branch/UTXO context, and any fixed jointly generated additional capsules. Classical PPT Recover(V,w) returns exactly the SAME K (except negligible error) for **every** valid ORIGINAL witness R(x,w)=1. States s_w may differ. For each false x, assume **full-seed one-wayness**: every nonuniform QPT A has negligible Pr[A(V)=K]. The adversary may inspect and coherently run all public programs. The public joint view, including correlations, is generated *before* the independently sampled mask below and must be replayable as the identical classical V for polynomially many reduction queries. The theorem also holds for polynomial input-independent quantum advice if the inverter receives polynomial copies as nonuniform advice. It does **not** cover uncloneable one-shot quantum side information correlated with K or a stateful, mask-responsive external oracle.

Define Enc(x,m) for one plaintext bit m: sample (V,K) honestly, choose r uniformly in {0,1}^h **after V**, return (V,r,c=m XOR <r,K> mod 2), then erase K. Dec((V,r,c),w)=c XOR <r,Recover(V,w)>. Every valid ORIGINAL witness decrypts the same m.

For a false x, if a QPT D guesses a uniform challenge plaintext M from (V,r,M XOR <r,K>) with advantage epsilon, define predictor P(V,r): sample independent uniform c, invoke D(V,r,c) to guess message, and return c XOR D(V,r,c). EXACTLY Pr[P(V,r)=<r,K>]=1/2+epsilon. By the Goldreich–Levin heavy-parity list decoder, polynomially many independent runs of P with the **same V and varied r** recover a polynomial list containing the exact K with nonnegligible probability whenever epsilon is nonnegligible. Randomly selecting a list member gives a nonnegligible full-seed inverter. The reduction calls QPT P on classical inputs with fresh independent invocations; no quantum rewinding. The mapping (K,setup randomness) -> V may be randomized; the parity decoder fixes V. Hence the one-bit WE IND advantage is negligible under this exact joint-view QPT one-wayness game.

This is an application of the established Goldreich–Levin theorem, not a new theorem in hardness amplification. See Goldreich–Levin (STOC 1989), doi:10.1145/73007.73010, and Adcock–Cleve (STACS 2002), arXiv:quant-ph/0108095. The latter analyzes further quantum black-box variants; this proof needs only rerunnable classical-output QPT prediction.

## Consequence and strict scope
For an NP-complete relation admitting polynomial witness-preserving reductions, the above compiler yields **bit-WE for arbitrary NP statements**. Thus a uniform general-NP local mixer promising even just false-instance full-seed one-wayness and all-witness SAME-capability correctness is already WE-capable. An assumption that simply asserts those properties is not independently shown weaker than WE. **This does not show that efficient implementations require iO, or rule out a relation-specific SYS/BTC primitive** for a non-NP-complete fixed relation. The reduction is not a practical construction: Gen is hypothetical. The Goldreich–Levin polynomial loss is not a 128-bit concrete security estimate.

Full-seed one-wayness is weaker than raw-key pseudorandomness: V may reveal some K bits while preventing full recovery. This is why a fresh independent parity mask is essential. A many-bit encryption cannot reuse one K for arbitrarily many exposed known-plaintext parity equations (h independent equations reconstruct K). The single-bit result does not claim general adaptive multi-capsule IND without a joint one-wayness game whose responses can be replayed for varied r.
