## 2. Actual refresh using the concrete MPC-in-head interface

One repetition uses the standard XOR-shared Boolean circuit execution described in the previous note, with independent salted hash commitments to views. A proof supplies commitments and one adjacent opened pair per repetition. A public operator cannot in general regenerate the omitted view from those bytes. For this attempt use the most favorable fixed-challenge simulator as a subroutine: given a prescribed adjacent-pair index a and random coins s, create consistent opened views for that index and commit to them, choose the unopened commitment in the simulator's prescribed manner. Grant its existence and correct fixed-challenge distribution for purposes of this refresh analysis; this is not a proof of the real MPC protocol's ZK property.

Concrete refresh U_j(p): take a subset I of repetitions to replace, retain all other commitments/openings from p, select desired new indices for I from e,j, and simulate replacements with common seed-derived coins. Query the joint challenge on the resulting commitment vector. If it equals the old indices outside I and the desired indices inside I, and verification succeeds, return the resulting proof; otherwise return p unchanged. Permit polynomial retries with distinct simulated commitments and arbitrary adaptive selection of I, indices and coins. This is an actual classical-PPT transition given the fixed-challenge simulator. All-reset uses I=all, so the new proposed bytes are independent of the starting proof when the target indices and coins depend only on e,j. Partial reset has dependence on the current proof through unchanged bytes.

For a previously unqueried commitment vector and exactly one available response per repetition fixed before the fresh joint challenge, the entire desired challenge vector is fixed conditional on the history. Its match probability is exactly 3^(-k). This holds even for partial reset: replacing one commitment changes the global hash input; the unchanged repetitions do not acquire alternate openings just because their own commitments are unchanged. With at most q distinct fresh challenge queries, the probability of any successful fresh refresh is at most q*3^(-k) by the conditional union bound. More generously, if the operator has openings prepared for a_i of the three indices at coordinate i before the fresh query, the probability of matching those sets is product_i(a_i/3). If at least m coordinates have a_i<=2, this is at most (2/3)^m. These bounds concern the specified preprepared-response refresh algorithm; they do NOT bound a general algorithm that can compute new responses adaptively after seeing the challenge. No QPT extraction or hash binding theorem is inferred from these probabilities.

Previously queried vectors need care. The initial accepted proof's vector is a known successful query, so an unchanged vector is not a fresh random trial. Returning it (and the same openings) is not a reset. Reusing a prior successful proposal is permissible but cannot increase the probability of finding a new successful vector among fresh trials. This calculation counts fresh proposals made by the refresh operator; it is not a claim about arbitrary correlated source advice containing extra oracle answers.

Consequently, for k growing at least linearly with lambda and polynomial q, with overwhelming probability F_e is the identity on the honest inputs in the actual one-opening refresh design. Build and Open independently generate proof randomness. Even if their transcript distributions were identical, equality of independently sampled proofs is not forced: in the concrete salted generator at least one revealed salt per repetition is lambda-bit random before challenge selection. For any fixed target transcript, equality requires that at least one of three independently sampled salts in the first repetition equals the corresponding target salt (or one of its two revealed salts). A conservative upper bound is 6*2^(-lambda) by union bound, regardless of which pair is opened. Conditional on fixed independent initial proof bytes, the same bound applies to the other prover's fresh salts. Thus identity plus rare fresh refresh fails the required equality with overwhelming probability in this experiment. This argument does not rely on support inequality between witnesses, a public list of final candidates, or the exponential minimum search.

## 3. Two exact repairs and why not to implement them

A. Replace the joint challenge by independent per-repetition hashes. Each fixed-index simulation can then retry independently until its local hash matches, with expected three trials. This changes the coupling drastically: every coordinate can be reset to a common proof in polynomial time. But the entire reset transcript can be constructed from public e and the public simulator WITHOUT an original proof or trapdoor. Repeating the local simulation also generates a fresh accepted transcript without a witness under this verification policy. A public adversary runs precisely the all-reset routine and derives H_key and the native mask. With bounded retries, success probability is at least 1-k*(2/3)^b for b independent attempts per coordinate. This is a concrete attack on this modified algorithm, not an attack on a correctly jointly hashed FS proof system.

B. Keep the joint hash but freeze the challenge vector to a public seed-derived value throughout all refreshes. Again all coordinates can reset under the prescribed indices, but the fixed-challenge simulator publicly supplies every response. The final proof and mask are public functions of e, ctx and h. If instead the challenge is frozen to the initial proof's hash and not independently published, it depends on the initial proof and need not agree across the setup/source starts. More importantly, if the terminal verifier accepts a challenge asserted by the prover without verifying the current commitment vector, the prover can assert any vector and simulate it. If the verifier retains the original FS transcript as an anchor, then terminal proof bytes still carry the differing initial proof unless the anchor is dropped from key derivation; dropping it while hashing only the common public reset bytes returns to public key recovery. This last claim is scoped to the specified transcript/key format, not all possible proofs of rerandomization.

The useful structural distinction for next selection is a **public transition whose valid-response capacity depends on an authentic hidden initial proof, but whose coalesced result does not retain that proof's identity**. A transition must change binding commitments while maintaining global source-proof consistency, without reducing to public fixed-challenge simulation. A true witness-dependent transition might answer fresh global challenges by retaining all witness data, but simply regenerating fresh MPC views with common coins does not establish the required cross-witness equality. Neither the actual refresh maps nor these two exact repairs provide that operation. Do not continue with different refresh schedules, more repetitions, longer seeds, more fixtures or challenge alphabets under the same preprepared-response rule.
