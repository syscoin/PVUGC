## 2. Conditional explicit shell

Sample t uniformly from {0,1}^lambda; let y=H("orbit-dummy",ctx,t). Use the OR relation Q_x,y(z) with either (0,w) satisfying R_x or (1,t') opening y in that hash domain. Grant a polynomial witness-preserving Hamiltonian-cycle compiler with graph G and public encoder Enc and decoder Dec: every Q witness yields a Hamiltonian cycle, and every Hamiltonian cycle decodes to a Q witness. The compiler must in addition produce a polynomial-size complete stabilizer chain for Aut(G), described below. These extra properties are NOT supplied by an ordinary NP reduction. Build knows t and can construct D=Enc(1,t) without any original w.

Require the following new structural correctness property for this compiler: for every qualifying original w, Enc(0,w) and D are in the SAME Aut(G) orbit on unoriented Hamiltonian cycles. This is NOT equality of cycles or equality of decoded witness tags. It is also not a property of arbitrary Hamiltonian-cycle encodings.

Build chooses a uniform permutation pi of the N graph vertices, publishes G'=pi(G) and the marked cycle L=pi(D) in G'. Let f_* be the lexicographically least permutation in the isomorphism coset Iso(G,G')=pi Aut(G). Set K=enc(f_*), where the representation is injective, and E=S XOR ExpandH("orbit-pad",ctx,K,|S|). Publish G, G', L, the group chain, Q-related public parameters and the exact native context, together with E. Never publish D, t or pi. For this shell assume honest polynomial-time generation of the graph and chain; no hidden evaluator is used.

Open with qualifying w:
  1. Compute C=Enc(0,w).
  2. Enumerate the 2N rotations and reversals aligning the ordered cyclic traversal of C with L. Each alignment specifies an entire bijection f of vertices. Check all adjacency/nonadjacency entries under f.
  3. Take any valid f. Canonicalize f Aut(G) as below to obtain f_*.
  4. Output E XOR ExpandH("orbit-pad",ctx,enc(f_*),|S|).

The orbit premise guarantees at least one valid alignment: if h(C)=D with h in Aut(G), then pi h maps G to G' and C to L, hence is one of the enumerated alignments. ANY valid graph isomorphism from G to G' belongs to pi Aut(G), even if it maps a different orientation. Thus canonicalization removes dependence on the particular witness and the alignment. It gives EXACT same native S for every original w under the premises, without hoping for hash collisions or using an independent setup opening in Open. Work is polynomial (at most 2N complete adjacency checks plus canonicalization and the real original verifier/encoder). A public verifier check is not the security enforcement.

Canonicalization is an actual public combinatorial computation conditional on a supplied complete chain. Fix domain vertex order 1..N, A_i the subgroup fixing 1..i, A_0=Aut(G). For each i give the orbit O_i of i under A_(i-1) and, for every a in O_i, a representative tau_i,a in A_(i-1) taking i to a. This is polynomial data: at most N^2 permutations of length N. Given a representative f, for i=1..N choose a in O_i minimizing f(a), then replace f by f composed with tau_i,a. Earlier images are fixed; all remaining representatives giving these first i images form precisely f A_i. Induction shows the final f is the lexicographic minimum over the original coset, with no enumeration of its potentially exponential membership. Build uses the same algorithm from pi. A supplied list of genuine automorphisms alone is not a proof of completeness of the chain; correctness under malicious setup would need actual chain verification or a compiler yielding a provably complete chain. A subgroup alone is insufficient for arbitrary-isomorphism canonical equality.

This changes the earlier special-soundness orientation in an important way. Dec(C) is not the desired native output; instead C is used to compute a witness-independent coset key. The key encodes an entire valid isomorphism, not a small invariant such as the parity of a cycle. No other proof transcript has to be explained.

## 3. Arbitrary-recovery extraction for the pad layer, and the exact unresolved reduction

Suppose an algorithm learns K. Decode f_*; verify it is a graph isomorphism. Since L is a Hamiltonian cycle in G', f_*^{-1}(L) is a Hamiltonian cycle in G. Dec of that cycle supplies either a genuine original certificate (0,w') or a dummy-domain preimage (1,t'). This is deterministic, with no rewinding or representation volunteered by the attacker.

For this exact shell there is a useful conditional QROM reduction from an arbitrary recovery advantage to K extraction, not just from explicit K output. Isolate the independent ideal orbit-pad domain; assume setup, native context, graph transcript and auxiliary state fixed independently of that domain. For a single capsule, let E be uniform of length |S| in the baseline and use a random oracle O. In the real experiment program the pad-stream addresses (ctx,K,i) for the requisite block indices to the corresponding blocks of E XOR S (for a partial block program only the used bits and sample the others). Conditional on setup this has exactly the real pad/ciphertext distribution, since uniform E makes the programmed values uniform independent by block. Keep all non-pad hash domains unchanged. This is an ideal-oracle construction, not a theorem about concrete SHA. It also does not cover auxiliary states that already depend on target pad-oracle values.

For a q-query arbitrary QPT attacker, let p_i be the baseline pre-query probability mass on the set of programmed addresses at query i (count zero for calls to other domains). Purify internal randomness; compare final states by telescoping query hybrids with baseline prefix and programmed suffix. Each oracle difference at query i has norm at most 2 sqrt(p_i); suffix unitaries preserve that norm. Trace-distance/event advantage is consequently at most 2 sum_i sqrt(p_i), averaged over setup and oracle. By Cauchy-Schwarz/Jensen, a real-vs-baseline event gap delta implies mean (sum_i p_i)/q >= delta^2/(4q^2). Run the BASELINE experiment up to a uniformly selected query and measure its input once; the probability of observing a target address is exactly that mean. Parse its K and verify/decode as above. No need to continue the measured attacker. This yields an actual QPT extraction algorithm for this conditional experiment, even when the attacker never outputs a representation. The baseline for exact S recovery or accepted native forgery is a separate native-security experiment; it cannot simply be set to zero, and same-message alternatives need explicit treatment. The bound also needs q>0; with zero pad queries the two experiments give identical output distributions under the independence premise.

IMPORTANT: extracting a dummy preimage is NOT yet a reduction to the independent hash assumption. In the preimage game the reduction receives y but does not know t. The shell needs t to generate D, then L. Giving the attacker (G',L) may leak t in a way absent in the plain preimage challenge. It is invalid to invoke hash preimage hardness on that correlated public view. Even if the graph compiler and complete group chain existed, the necessary simulator would need to sample the full marked graph distribution from y, without the unknown dummy preimage and without extracting the original witness first.

There is an exact orbit fact that isolates this missing simulator. For fixed G and cycles C,D in the same orbit, the distribution of (pi(G),pi(C)) for uniform pi equals that of (pi(G),pi(D)): compose the uniform pi with an automorphism mapping C to D. Thus an already-known ORIGINAL w can replace t in sampling the marked cycle. This is not a reduction on FALSE instances (there is no such w), nor a reduction in the hard-TRUE case where the reduction is not given the qualifying certificate. A sampler that simply takes a known alternative witness is not sufficient. Sampling only G' and a uniform unrelated L does not preserve the condition that L is a cycle in G' or the joint orbit distribution. Publishing the isomorphism to solve the sampling problem makes the full key public. These are exact distribution/correlation gaps, not a parameter choice.

## 4. Compiler repair screens

Standard witness-preserving cycle reductions need not give the stated orbit transitivity. In particular, any compiler whose branch tag is invariant under all graph automorphisms cannot satisfy it: Dec(C) has tag 0 on an encoded original witness, Dec(D) tag 1 on the encoded dummy witness, contradicting invariance if they share an orbit. Ordinary structurally distinguished selector gadgets can have this defect. A compiler could use vertex labels in the decoder so that automorphisms change decoded tags; this avoids that immediate contradiction but does not construct the required automorphisms.

Consider deriving K from the images of individually fixed anchor vertices rather than the full coset. This removes the need for full group canonicalization, but loses the explicit arbitrary-recovery extractor: a tuple of anchor images is not in general an isomorphism. If all anchors are identified by efficiently computable unique graph invariants (e.g. unique degrees), their images are publicly computable and the pad is recovered without an original witness. With ALL vertices individually fixed, Aut(G) is trivial and the orbit premise forces C=D for every encoded witness, impossible with deterministic distinct branch tags. Thus the full canonical coset, not a small public anchor invariant or a rigid graph, is the relevant key in the shell.

A direct repair would publish a special labeled graph whose cycles lie in one orbit with an explicit chain and whose permuted marked graph has a sampler given only (x,y). Such a compiler/sampler must preserve witness extraction on *every* accepted cycle and cannot assume the existence of an original witness on FALSE. It is not implemented here; relying on hardness of graph isomorphism, or on a hidden sampler that returns the desired witness-dependent isomorphism, is not an allowed independent hash-only foundation. No claim of impossibility of all graph constructions follows.

For this candidate the remaining operations are precise and coupled:
 (a) generate the original-relation graph with a planted dummy and complete group chain, all ORIGINAL cycles orbit-compatible with the planted cycle;
 (b) jointly simulate (G,G',L,chain,y,context) from an independent hash challenge without a dummy opening or a known source witness, and extend to actual correlated source/native/sibling capsules and auxiliary oracle access.
Without (a), even the honest polynomial alignment is unproved for any original bridge evidence. Without (b), the measured-query extraction ends in a preimage with unaccounted correlated advice. No concrete native signature or malicious N-of-N setup, no proof of full FALSE/hard-TRUE security. Do not treat the conditional coset transport or isolated oracle argument as the missing advance.
