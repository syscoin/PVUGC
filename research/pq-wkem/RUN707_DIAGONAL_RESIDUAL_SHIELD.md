## New fixed-matrix diagonal rank lemma (full proof)

For ANY fixed A in Mat_N(F_q), D=diag(d_1,...,d_N) with independent uniform field entries, and 1<=s<=N,
 Pr[rank(A+D)<=N-s] <= T(q,N,s) <= binom(N,s) q^(-s),
 T(q,N,s)=q^(-N) sum_{j=0}^{N-s} binom(N,j)(q-1)^j.
The footprint bound is SHARP for A=0.

Proof: let h=N-s+1 and form ideal J in F_q[d_1,...,d_N], generated by every principal h-by-h minor det((A+D)_{I,I}) for |I|=h, and by d_i^q-d_i for every i. The unique highest-total-degree monomial of each principal minor is product_{i in I} d_i, with coefficient 1. Therefore under a graded term order its leading monomial is this product; the field equations lead with d_i^q. Every standard monomial in F_q[d]/J has exponents <q and support size at most h-1=N-s. There are at most sum_{j<=N-s} binom(N,j)(q-1)^j such monomials. Modulo all finite-field equations the polynomial ring is the algebra of functions on F_q^N; the dimension of its quotient by J is exactly the number of common rational zeros of J. Every diagonal d for which rank(A+D)<=N-s annihilates every such principal minor, so its count is at most this standard-monomial number. Dividing by q^N yields T. The last inequality follows by the union bound for at least s zero entries among N independent uniform field elements. For A=0, the rank event is exactly at least s zeros, and the footprint count equals it. QED.
