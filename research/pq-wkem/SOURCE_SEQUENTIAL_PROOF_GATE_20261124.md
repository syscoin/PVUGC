## 1. Trial interface

Let R_x be the unchanged bounded ORIGINAL source relation with the three independent OR grounds and full authentication, relevance and maturity. For the test grant an efficient reduction to a graph G_x whose proper 3-colorings can be computed from ORIGINAL witnesses. Grant soundness/extraction of that reduction as well; no actual graph compiler or QPT extractor is claimed. The trial uses a classical interactive proof internal to local Open. Its transcript is not posted on Bitcoin. The publicly serialized C would hold state z_0, and public classical transition algorithms would expose:

    Challenge(z_i,commitments_i) -> (edge_i,z'_i)
    Respond(z'_i,endpoint_openings_i) -> z_(i+1) or reject
    Finish(z_r) -> S or reject

S is the precommitted exact native cancellation capability. Open runs a prover with a coloring from its original source witness, interacting with these functions for r sequential repetitions. Build must generate z_0 from x,S before witnesses exist; no party remains online. All state bytes and transition algorithms are copyable/replayable, even if a trial compiler makes their semantic contents opaque. Random coins can be sampled as required and retained at a snapshot. Challenge outputs an edge of the graph; the only prover response is the two endpoint openings for that edge. There is a fresh independently randomized coloring commitment in each repetition. Any additional proof-dependent message or hidden hardware state would be a different interface and is not granted. This proposal's hoped-for advantage is that Finish need not use the proof bytes as a mask key, so prior minimum selection, coalescence and public inverse objections do not by themselves establish failure.

For a concrete hash-only commitment in this candidate, each repetition commits to all n vertices: choose a fresh uniform permutation of the three colors and independent lambda-bit salts a_v, set c_v=H_commit(ctx,round,v,a_v,color_v). On challenge (u,v), reveal the two salts and colors. Check commitments and inequality. Use an independent domain from the actual source hashes and any unrelated key derivation. For this screening analysis H_commit is a classical random oracle, not a programmable concrete SHA claim. The graph has m>=1 edges; n,m,r and all public transition costs are polynomial in the security parameter. All prior queries to this independent domain by setup/source/auxiliary generation are assumed polynomial and the proof salts are fresh independent thereafter. A fixed-instance experiment with independent nonuniform advice is also permitted. No conclusion is made for arbitrary advice dependent on an exponentially queried proof commitment oracle.

## 2. Direct public replay attack in a hard-TRUE experiment

Assume the graph is colorable but original evidence is computationally inaccessible from the full baseline public inputs (apart from any alleged release leakage). The attack never computes a coloring or original witness. It receives the actual C, including the real setup context and all correlations; no simulator of Build is needed for this attack. It executes the actual public transition code, not a modified verifier or a guessed output key.

At each round snapshot z_i. For one trial choose an edge e* uniformly among m edges. Pick a uniform ordered pair of distinct colors for its endpoints, arbitrary independent uniform colors for all other vertices, and fresh salts. Compute all n commitments exactly as above. Run Challenge on the snapshot and these commitments. If its edge is not e*, discard its tentative state and retry from z_i. Otherwise open the two committed endpoints and run Respond on that successful trial state. After r rounds execute Finish. Limit trials to b per round; all computations are classical PPT. There is no query to a proof simulator oracle, no requested internal state or representation of an attacker, and no observation or rewinding of a QPT adversary. Snapshotting the public classical evaluator is part of the attacker's own local computation. The hash oracle is not rewound.

Here is the probability/distribution argument for the ideal-hash interface, including a challenge that depends arbitrarily on all commitment bytes and earlier round states. In the experiment with fresh hidden salt inputs and independent uniform commitment outputs, the commitments and the challenge derived from them are independent of e*. For any valid edge output of Challenge, the probability of a hit is 1/m, even if the challenge is not uniform. Conditional on a hit, the joint distribution of (commitment vector, edge, opened endpoint colors and salts) is exactly the honest distribution in that idealized experiment: conditioning has the same factor 1/m for each commitment vector and chosen edge, and under a uniform color permutation the honest colors at any fixed edge are uniformly distributed over the six ordered distinct pairs. Their salts are independent uniform. The unseen colors at other vertices need not agree with a proper coloring because the interface never opens them in that repetition.

The idealized experiment is related to the stated classical random-oracle experiment by deferred exposure of independently salted commitment inputs. Until an unopened salt is queried or collides with a prior input, the unseen hash outputs are independent uniform and conceal their color inputs. Once a salt is opened, answer its prescribed hash consistently; the actual attack simply computes hashes in the real oracle and uses fresh salts on each retry. This is a proof coupling, not an oracle-programming step in the attack. Count all commitment slots across real and simulated runs and retries by T and all other classical queries in both coupled executions and prior generation by q. A conservative union bound for unwanted salt exposure/reuse is O(T*(q+T)/2^lambda), by guessing a fresh lambda-bit salt before exposure, including the adaptive queries following endpoint revelation. The domains include the round and vertex; ignoring this extra separation only enlarges the bound. If some externally provided correlated advice already includes an opened salt, its contribution must be charged or independence re-established; the stated fresh-salt experiment excludes that dependence. Unopened commitment slots never receive a legitimate opening later; retries are fresh attempts. Hash oracle query logs are not part of Challenge's input. Polynomially many failed trials do not change the random oracle's fixed values on fresh distinct inputs. Snapshot replay does not restore or program the hash oracle.

In the idealized experiment the chance that any round exhausts b trials is at most r*(1-1/m)^b <= r*exp(-b/m). Use b=ceil(m*(lambda+ln(max(1,r)))) for an exponentially small cap failure. If the actual capsule has honest correctness 1-eps for the original prover, the simulated accepted transcript/state distribution yields the same exact S with probability at least 1-eps-r*exp(-b/m)-O(T*(q+T)/2^lambda) in the stated experiment. This follows by sequential coupling of the successful round transcripts and the actual evaluator coins. The formula assumes Challenge always outputs a valid edge, as the trial interface specifies; an abort-capable implementation would need to account for the honest abort event too. Finish returns S, not merely a valid proof, so checking a native VK is not needed to identify a candidate among many. This is an actual classical unauthorized hard-TRUE recovery strategy for any instantiation of that precise interface with the asserted correctness. It does not establish FALSE security or break hash preimage/collision resistance. It remains valid if the public code conceals state semantics, because copying serialized state is sufficient.

## 3. Repair check before any implementation

Two potential changes affect the attack rather than increasing fixture size. First, commit to all repetitions and use one challenge vector across them, with no opportunity to rerandomize a failed single repetition. The per-vector guessed response method then has match probability m^(-r) in the independent uniform guess experiment. This removes the efficient replay strategy above but returns to the global-challenge proof interface: acceptance of a proof is not an implemented secret-bearing transition. The earlier joint-challenge coalescence note already ruled out using simulated per-coordinate replacement as a common key. Do not implement that same refresh or merely change challenge alphabet. This run does not rule out a genuinely new joint-challenge secret-release compiler.

Second, open more than one edge of the *same* fixed commitment, until all relevant edges are checked. Independent endpoint simulations no longer have the required consistency. Full coverage directly discloses a coloring (isolated vertices may be assigned arbitrarily); a genuine reduction can then give an ORIGINAL witness. This abandons the simulatable transcript interface and does not rely on a common proof object. However it leaves exactly the unconstructed operation: how the serialized public state, made before future witnesses exist, changes from inaccessible S to accessible S on that globally bound witness. A public hash check followed by an accessible plaintext/output seed is not a cryptographic gate, and hiding it in a public program is not an algorithm or independent assumption. Garbling with unrestricted input labels would revisit the known cross-witness switching regressions, so do not instantiate that unchanged variant. In particular neither the mere cost of rewinding a joint proof nor special soundness of a complete opening supplies a secret-release compiler.

## 4. Handoff

For selection, distinguish source authentication with a simulatable proof transcript from a source-dependent release correlation: local replay can generate the former without evidence in the short-challenge interface, even if the proposed compiler avoids all common-transcript equalities. A viable alternative needs a non-simulatable source-dependent input to actual PPT public release transitions, a method to construct those transitions from x,S alone, and a full joint security argument (not merely a proof-of-knowledge protocol). Do not promote the conditional replay attack to a general impossibility of source-specific WE, an impossibility of all NIZKs, or a QROM theorem.
